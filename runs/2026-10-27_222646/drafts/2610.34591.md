Subject: Question regarding your paper on Context Halo effects

Dear Authors,

I am Pranav Yadav, an MSc student at the University of Bonn. I am writing to ask a specific question about your paper, "Does the Outfit Change the Price? Context Halo in Vision-Language Model Valuation of an Identical Low-Cost Necklace."

My current project investigates finding the attention heads that causally produce refusal in small instruct LLMs using activation patching and LoRA editing. Specifically, we are examining whether such edits produce substantive harmful content or only alter the refusal opener. Your finding that a single instruction can significantly reduce context-driven valuation effects, while other prompts fail to do so, offers a compelling parallel to our investigation of superficial versus substantive behavioral changes.

Given that your text-only task replicated the visual halo effect, does this suggest that the mechanism driving these valuation shifts is purely linguistic context rather than visual perception? I am interested in whether this distinction holds when the context is explicitly manipulated via instruction versus inherent in the input modality.
